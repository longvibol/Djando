1- get from id 

>>> Book.objects.get(id=3)
<Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>


2- get from field 

>>> Book.objects.get(title="My Story") 
<Book: My Story (2)>

Ex:

>>> Book.objects.all()
<QuerySet [<Book: Lord of the Rings (4)>, <Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>, <Book: My Story (2)>, <Book: Some random book (1)>]>
>>> Book.objects.get(rating=5)
<Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>


3- filter (equal =)

>>> Book.objects.filter(is_bestselling=True)
<QuerySet [<Book: Lord of the Rings (4)>, <Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>]>


or 

>>> Book.objects.filter(is_bestselling=False) 
<QuerySet [<Book: My Story (2)>, <Book: Some random book (1)>]>



4- filter (big or smaller)

>>> Book.objects.filter(rating__lt=3)
<QuerySet [<Book: My Story (2)>, <Book: Some random book (1)>]>

Note : we use __ user score two times : rating__lt=3

Link to check for the : django filter

https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/6.0/topics/db/queries/


5- filter with two parameter : title__contains="My Story",rating__lt=3

>>> Book.objects.get(title__contains="My Story",rating__lt=3) 
<Book: My Story (2)>

6- Egnore case sensitive : __icontains

>>> Book.objects.get(title__icontains="Story",rating__lt=3) 
<Book: My Story (2)>

7- or query : import Django. Q

from django.db.models import Q
>>> Book.objects.filter(Q(rating__lt=3) | Q(is_bestselling=True))
<QuerySet [<Book: Lord of the Rings (4)>, <Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>, <Book: My Story (2)>, <Book: Some random book (1)>]>


note we use (|) for the or query 


Ex: with and (,) comma 

>>> Book.objects.filter(Q(rating__lt=3) | Q(is_bestselling=True), author="vibol")        
<QuerySet [<Book: Lord of the Rings (4)>]>


Note: we use (or) before and 


8- scope of the filter 

>>> bestsellers = Book.objects.filter(is_bestselling=True)
>>> amazing_bestsellers = bestsellers.filter(rating__gt=4)
>>> print(bestsellers)
<QuerySet [<Book: Lord of the Rings (4)>, <Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>]>
>>> print(amazing_bestsellers)
<QuerySet [<Book: Harry Potter 1 (5)>]>
>>>
